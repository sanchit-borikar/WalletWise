# Credit card basics (India)

## How a credit card works
A credit card lets you spend the bank's money now and pay it back by the due date with no interest. Pay the full bill every month and the card gives you rewards, cashback and purchase protection at no cost. Pay late or only the minimum and interest of about 40% a year wipes out every reward.

## Interest-free period
You get free credit from the day you spend until the bill due date, usually 20 to 50 days. It works only if you pay the total amount due by the due date. If any part of the last bill is unpaid, new purchases start charging interest from the day you make them.

## Minimum amount due
The minimum due only keeps your account from being marked late. Interest still runs on everything you have not paid, at about 3 to 4% a month (roughly 40% a year), and you lose the interest-free period on new spends. Always pay the total amount due.

## Billing cycle and due date
Your card collects a month of spends, then the bank makes a statement. You then have at least about two weeks to pay before the due date. RBI rules let you change your billing date at least once, so you can set it a few days after your salary arrives.

## Credit score (CIBIL)
Your CIBIL score (300 to 900) shows lenders how reliably you repay. It rises when you pay every bill on time and use less than about 30% of your limit. Late payments hurt it most. A score above 750 usually gets the best cards and loans.

## Annual fee and fee waiver
Many cards charge a yearly fee plus 18% GST, but waive it if you spend a set amount in a year. A card is worth paying for only if its extra rewards beat the fee at your normal spending.

## Forex markup
When you pay in a foreign currency, most cards add a 2 to 3.5% markup plus 18% GST. On a ₹50,000 trip that can be ₹2,000 or more, often more than the rewards you earn. Use a low-forex card for international spends.

## Airport lounges
Many cards give free airport lounge visits, but more and more now require a minimum spend in the previous quarter before you can use them. Check your card's lounge rules.

## Points versus cashback
Cashback is money back on your bill. Points are worth whatever you redeem them for: the median Indian card point is worth about 25 paise, while some premium cards reach ₹1 or more, and transfers to airlines can be worth more again. Beginners usually do best with simple cashback.

## Transferring points to airlines and hotels
Some cards let you move points to airline or hotel programmes, often worth more than cash. Transfers cannot be reversed and ratios change often, so find the award seat first, then transfer only what you need.

## Devaluations
Banks often cut rewards, add caps or remove partners. RBI rules require at least a month of notice, and if a change hurts you, you can close the card without an extra closure charge once dues are paid.

## Credit cards on UPI
Only RuPay credit cards can be linked to UPI apps like Google Pay or PhonePe. You pay nothing extra; the merchant bears any charge. Payments up to ₹2,000 often earn no reward on credit-card UPI, so check your card terms.

## EMI and no-cost EMI
An EMI splits a purchase into monthly instalments, usually with interest and a processing fee. "No-cost" EMI means the discount covers the interest. RBI rules say interest-bearing EMIs cannot be called no-cost, so check the principal, interest and fee shown before you convert.

## Cash withdrawal from a credit card
Avoid it. Cash withdrawals carry a fee of about 2.5% and interest from day one, with no interest-free period.

## Credit limit
Your limit is the most you can owe at once. Using less than about 30% of it helps your credit score. Banks cannot raise your limit or upgrade your card without your explicit consent.

## Closing a card
Banks must close a card within seven working days of your request once dues are paid, or pay you ₹500 a day for the delay. Closing an old card can lower your score a little because it reduces your total limit and credit history.
