# Your rights and card scams (India)

## Who these rules cover
The RBI Credit Cards and Debit Cards Directions, 2025 apply to commercial banks and SBI (para 3). Small finance banks, payments banks and local area banks follow a separate set of directions. In WalletWise, cards from AU, Utkarsh, Unity and slice are small finance bank cards, so these rules may differ for them: check with the bank. This is a plain-language summary, not legal advice.

## Unsolicited card or upgrade
A bank may not issue a card or upgrade a card without the customer's explicit consent (para 11(4)), and may not upgrade a card or raise the credit limit on its own (para 34). If you are billed for a card or upgrade you did not ask for, the bank must reverse the charges at once and pay you a penalty of twice the amount reversed (para 11(4)). You can also go to the RBI Ombudsman for compensation. Do not activate such a card or give an OTP for it; without your consent the bank must close it at no cost to you. Any loss from misuse of an unsolicited card is the bank's responsibility, not yours (para 11(5)).

## Closing a card
Once your dues are paid, the bank must close your card within seven working days. If it is late, it must pay you ₹500 for each calendar day of delay (para 19). The bank must accept a closure request by helpline, email, IVR, website, internet banking or app, and cannot insist on a request by post.

## Closing after a change that hurts you
Changes in charges apply only from a future date, with at least one month's notice. If a change in charges is to your disadvantage, you can surrender the card without any extra closure charge, once your dues are paid (para 23(7)). Other terms can change with 30 days' notice (para 79). RBI rules do not stop banks from cutting rewards; your protection is the notice and the right to close.

## Key Fact Statement and rejection reasons
With every credit card application the bank must give a one-page Key Fact Statement showing the interest rate and charges. If it rejects an application, it must tell you the specific reason in writing (para 11(1)).

## Wrong bills and fraud disputes
If you dispute a bill, the bank must give an explanation, with documents where needed, within 30 days of your complaint (para 25). No charges may be levied on a transaction you have disputed as fraud until the dispute is resolved (para 27).

## Changing your billing date
You can change your card's billing cycle at least once, to any start or closing date you like (para 29). Many people set it so the payment due date falls a few days after salary day.

## Lost or stolen card
The bank must block a lost card as soon as you tell it (para 68), and must give several ways to report loss, theft or misuse, such as a helpline, SMS, email, website and app (para 69).

## Complaints and the RBI Ombudsman
Complain to the bank first and keep the complaint number. If the bank does not reply within 30 days, rejects your complaint, or you are not satisfied with its answer, you can complain to the RBI Ombudsman online at https://cms.rbi.org.in (para 85). It is free. The bank must compensate you for loss of time, expenses, financial loss and harassment caused by its fault (para 85). ## Common card scams
- "Your reward points expire today" SMS with a link: the link leads to a fake site that steals card details and OTPs. Check points only in the bank's own app.
- An APK file sent on WhatsApp or SMS to update KYC, redeem points or activate a card: these apps read your SMS and steal OTPs. Install bank apps only from the Play Store or App Store.
- A "customer care" number found through a search engine, ad or social media: scammers post fake helplines. Use only the number on the back of your card or in the bank's app.
- A caller who asks you to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer or another screen-sharing app: they can then see your OTPs and bank app.
- An upgrade, limit-increase or fee-waiver call that asks for the OTP you just received: an OTP approves a payment. Banks need your consent for upgrades and never ask for an OTP on a call.
- A fake KYC or "card will be blocked" message: ignore the link and call the bank on its official number.
- A "refund" that needs you to scan a QR code or enter your UPI PIN: you never need a PIN to receive money.

## What to do if you are scammed
Never share your OTP, CVV, card PIN, UPI PIN or full card number with anyone, even someone who says they are from the bank. If you already have: block the card at once in the bank's app or by calling the number on the back of the card; call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 as soon as possible; report at cybercrime.gov.in; and tell the bank in writing that the transactions are fraud, so it cannot charge you for them while the dispute is open (para 27). Keep screenshots, call logs and the complaint number.
