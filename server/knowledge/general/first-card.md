# Getting your first credit card (no credit history)

## Who this is for
Students, homemakers, gig workers, freelancers and people in their first job often have no credit card, no loan and no CIBIL score yet. Many banks turn down a first application because they cannot see a history or an income proof. There are three safe ways to start.

## Route 1: a card against a fixed deposit (FD-backed or secured card)
You open a fixed deposit with the bank and it gives you a credit card whose limit is close to the FD amount. The bank marks a lien on the FD as security. Approval is almost certain, no salary slip or ITR is usually needed, and the FD keeps earning interest. If you do not pay the bill, the bank can recover the money from the FD. Paying the full bill on time builds your CIBIL score exactly like any other card.

Minimum FD stated on each bank's own page (checked 2026-10-20):
- IDFC FIRST WOW!: ₹20,000. No credit history or income proof needed. Lifetime free, zero forex markup.
- IDFC FIRST WOW! Black: ₹20,000. No credit history or income proof needed.
- IDFC FIRST EA₹N (RuPay, virtual): ₹5,000. No credit history or income proof needed. Works on UPI.
- IDFC FIRST Hello Cashback (RuPay): ₹10,000. No credit history or income proof needed.
- Kotak 811 #DreamDifferent: ₹10,000 for at least 181 days. No income proof or CIBIL score needed. No annual fee; joining fee ₹500 including GST from 1 Sept 2026.
- AU NOMO: ₹20,000. No documents or income proof needed. One-time joining fee ₹199, free from the second year.
- SBI Card Unnati: an SBI fixed deposit of ₹25,000 or more. Annual fee ₹499 waived for the first four years.
- super.money superCard (Utkarsh Small Finance Bank, RuPay): minimum deposit ₹100. No joining or annual fee. Works on UPI.
- Canara Bank RuPay Platinum Secured and RuPay Select Secured: limit up to 75% of the FD, minimum card limit ₹25,000.

## Route 2: an add-on card on a family member's card
A parent, spouse or sibling who already has a credit card can ask their bank for an add-on card in your name. It shares their credit limit and their bill. The main cardholder is responsible for everything you spend. An add-on card usually does not build your own credit score, because the account is in their name. Ask for a spending limit on the add-on card and never share OTPs.

## Route 3: a free or low-fee entry card
If you have a salary slip or ITR, an entry card with no annual fee (or a small fee that is waived at your spend) and a low income minimum can be a good first card. The bank checks your income and credit history, so a first application can be refused. Apply for one card at a time: several applications close together lower your score. Avoid cards with high annual fees until you know you will use the benefits.

## Building your CIBIL score with your first card
1. Pay the total amount due every month, by the due date. Set up auto-debit.
2. Keep your bill under about 30% of your credit limit.
3. Do not apply for many cards at once. Wait at least six months between applications.
4. Get your free credit report every year from each of the four credit bureaus (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark).
5. Dispute any mistake on the bureau's website, and ask the bank to correct it.
6. Keep your oldest card open, especially if it has no annual fee.

## Warnings
- Never pay anyone who promises a card "without checks" or to "fix" your score. Never share an OTP, PIN or CVV.
- Paying only the minimum due costs about 3 to 4% a month in interest and hurts your score.
- After six to twelve months of on-time payments, many banks offer an unsecured card. You can then close the FD-backed card, but keeping your oldest card helps your score.
